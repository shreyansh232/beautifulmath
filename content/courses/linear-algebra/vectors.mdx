## First principles

A **vector** can be:

1. A geometric arrow (direction + length)
2. A coordinate list $(v_1, v_2, \ldots, v_n)$

<VectorDiagram />

Addition tip-to-tail matches componentwise sums. Scaling $c\mathbf{v}$ stretches (or flips if $c < 0$).


## Slow down: arrows that add

Tip-to-tail addition is a physical story: walk one displacement, then another. The shortcut home is the sum. Componentwise addition is the same story told in coordinates. When data scientists say "vector," they often mean the coordinate list - but the arrow intuition still teaches what scaling and adding do.

If $\mathbf{u}$ points east and $\mathbf{v}$ points north, $\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}$ points northeast with a length the Pythagorean theorem can compute. Geometry lesson, still paying rent.

## Why lists matter for data

One row of a spreadsheet is a vector. Features become axes. Distance and angle become geometry on data.

## Span (preview)

Linear combinations $a\mathbf{v} + b\mathbf{w}$ fill a line or a plane (or more) depending on how independent $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{w}$ are.

## Real world

Displacements add as vectors: walk east then north. Net displacement is the diagonal.

## AI / ML bridge

Word embeddings place tokens in high-dimensional space so "direction" can encode relations. The famous king - man + woman ≈ queen analogy is vector arithmetic on meaning (imperfect, but the geometry is the point).
