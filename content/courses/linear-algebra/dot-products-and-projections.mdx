## First principles

For $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$,

$$
\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v} = \sum_i u_i v_i = \|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\| \cos\theta
$$

when the geometric interpretation applies. Large positive: aligned. Near zero: orthogonal. Negative: opposing.


## Slow down: multiply matching parts

Dot product adds the products of corresponding components. Geometrically it secretly contains $\cos\phi$. Projection uses that number to drop a perpendicular shadow of one vector onto another - the core of least squares later.

## Projection

The shadow of $\mathbf{v}$ onto the direction of $\mathbf{u}$ is

$$
\mathrm{proj}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{v} = \left(\frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u}}\right)\mathbf{u}
$$

(for $\mathbf{u} \neq 0$). That is "how much of $\mathbf{v}$ lies along $\mathbf{u}$."

## Length

$\|\mathbf{v}\| = \sqrt{\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{v}}$.

## Real world

If you push a crate at an angle, only the component along the motion does work. Dot product filters the aligned part.

## AI / ML bridge

Cosine similarity is the dot product of normalized vectors. Attention logits are often scaled dot products between queries and keys.
