## How this idea was born

Coordinates feel absolute until you change them. Graphics frames, sensor arrays, and data tables all confront the same struggle: which independent directions are enough to describe everything you care about, and what stays true when you rewrite the labels?

**Emmy Noether** (1882-1935) rebuilt algebra around structure and homomorphism, often while institutions blocked her path. Students flocked to her anyway. MacTutor is the biographical stop. A **basis** is a minimal wardrobe that still dresses every vector uniquely. **Dimension** counts how many independent costume pieces you need.

For a modern rereader, this chapter is about structure that survives rewriting - why PCA, graphics frames, and change-of-basis drills keep returning. Without bases, coordinates feel absolute. With bases, you see the costume for what it is.

## First principles

Vectors $\mathbf{v}_1,\ldots,\mathbf{v}_k$ are **linearly independent** if the only solution to

$$
a_1\mathbf{v}_1 + \cdots + a_k\mathbf{v}_k = \mathbf{0}
$$

is all $a_i = 0$. No vector in the set is redundant.

A **basis** of a space is an independent spanning set: every vector has unique coordinates relative to that basis.

**Dimension** is the number of vectors in any basis (a theorem says they all agree).

The column space dimension of a matrix is its **rank**: how many independent output directions it can hit.

## Worked intuition

The same arrow can wear the costume $(1,0)$ in one basis and a different pair in another. A plane is two-dimensional because two independent directions span it - not because someone drew an $x$ and $y$ axis in a textbook.

This is why change of basis matters in graphics, PCA, and physics: choose axes that make the phenomenon simple. PCA seeks informative orthogonal directions of variance. CAD software lets you pick convenient frames for a part. Sensors that duplicate each other are dependent directions wearing hardware clothes.

Algebrica's vector spaces and change-of-basis pages reward patience. Dimension is not "number of coordinates on the page." It is the size of a basis.

Three mutually perpendicular unit axes basis ordinary space. Four legs of a table that impose coplanar constraints show dependence in physical disguise: more sticks than independent motions.

## Common confusions

**Dimension equals ambient list length.** A line through the origin in $\mathbb{R}^3$ is still one-dimensional even though each point wears three ambient coordinates. Dimension is about the subspace, not the ambient costume size.

**Any spanning set is a basis.** Spanning sets can be redundant. Bases are spanning and independent. Throw out extras until uniqueness of coordinates appears.

**Rank as a mysterious matrix property.** Rank is dimension of the column space (equivalently row space). It answers: how many independent output directions can this map produce?

**Coordinates as the object.** Change basis and coordinates change, but geometric relationships (when properly expressed) persist. Confusing the costume for the actor is the root bug.

**Ambient dimension worship.** More coordinates are not automatically more truth. Effective dimension of data may be far smaller.

**Confusing orthogonal with independent in casual speech.** Orthogonal is an inner-product story. Independence is a linear-combination story. Related in Euclidean settings with care; not synonyms everywhere.

## Real world

Choosing axes for a CAD model, or picking independent sensors that do not duplicate information, is choosing a useful basis. Robotics and computer vision change frames constantly: camera, robot, world.

Compression and dimensionality reduction are bets that data approximately live in a lower-dimensional structure inside a larger ambient space.

## AI / ML bridge

PCA and related methods find informative bases for data. Rank of a weight matrix limits how many independent feature directions a layer can produce. Low-rank bottlenecks (intentional or accidental) constrain capacity.

Embeddings live in a chosen dimension: enough axes to separate concepts, not so many that you drown in noise. "The" embedding dimension is a modeling choice about basis size for meaning space.

## For the rereader

After living with gradients, notice that parameter space dimension is the number of independent knobs - until constraints or ties reduce effective dimension. After probability, notice that a distribution over a discrete set of size $n$ lives in an $(n-1)$-dimensional simplex because probabilities sum to one: dependence built into the definition.

A sharper nuance: bases are not unique, but dimension is. Much of linear algebra's power is theorems that say "every basis has the same size" and "rank is well-defined." Invariants are the point.

## A longer structural studio

In $\mathbb{R}^2$, show that $\{(1,0),(0,1)\}$ and $\{(1,1),(1,-1)\}$ are both bases. Take the same geometric arrow and compute coordinates in each basis. Feel the costume change while the arrow stays.

Now take $\{(1,0),(2,0)\}$. Spanning a line, not independent. Throw one out to get a basis of that line. Redundancy versus minimality becomes tactile.

## Rank with a matrix

Write a $2\times3$ matrix whose two rows are multiples. Column space dimension collapses. Rank names that collapse. Low-rank layers in ML are capacity bottlenecks, sometimes intentional (compression), sometimes accidental (undertraining collapse).

## Extended examples

**Two bases, one arrow.** $\mathbf{v}=(2,2)$ in the standard basis. In basis $\mathbf{b}_1=(1,1)$, $\mathbf{b}_2=(1,-1)$, coordinates become $(2,0)$. Same arrow, new costume.

**Dependent sensors.** Two thermometers glued together produce dependent readings. Three independent axes in space do not. Hardware can illustrate linear dependence.

**Rank-nullability glimpse.** Dimension theorem ideas: dimensions of column space and null space add to number of columns (standard theorem). Capacity lost to the null space is invisible output variation.

**One-hot basis.** Standard basis vectors are the one-hot costumes. Embeddings learn more useful bases for meaning. Dimension choice is still a bet.

**Invariant checklist.** Lengths and angles are not invariant under every basis change unless you track the inner product carefully. Structure first; coordinates second.

## Glossary of moves

- **Independence:** no nontrivial combination gives zero.
- **Spanning set:** combinations fill the space.
- **Basis:** independent spanning set; unique coordinates.
- **Dimension:** size of any basis.
- **Change of basis:** new coordinates, same vector.
- **Rank:** dimension of column space.
- **Effective dimension:** useful degrees of freedom in data, often less than ambient.
