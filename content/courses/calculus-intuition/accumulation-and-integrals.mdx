## First principles

Slice an interval into thin widths $\Delta x$, height about $f(x_i)$, area about $f(x_i)\Delta x$. Sum the slices. As slices thin, the sum approaches the **definite integral** $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$ when the limit exists.

<AreaUnderCurveDiagram />

If $f$ is a rate, the integral accumulates net change.

## Antiderivatives

A function $F$ with $F' = f$ is an antiderivative of $f$. Adding a constant does not change the derivative, so antiderivatives come in families $F + C$.

## Fundamental theorem (intuition)

Differentiating an accumulation from a fixed start recovers the rate. Integrating a rate recovers net accumulation. Derivative and integral are inverse stories (under suitable conditions).

Archimedes's method of exhaustion is the ancient ancestor of "approximate, then refine."

## Real world

Speedometer readings over a trip, accumulated carefully, estimate distance. Gaps and sign (direction) matter.

## AI / ML bridge

Risk $= \mathbb{E}[\ell(y, f(x))]$ accumulates loss across the data-generating process. Mini-batches estimate that integral/sum.
