## First principles

For $f(x, y)$, the partial $\partial f/\partial x$ treats $y$ as fixed and differentiates in $x$. The **gradient**

$$
\nabla f = \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}, \frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)
$$

points to the direction of steepest ascent (when it exists and is nonzero). Steepest descent is $-\nabla f$.

## Why a vector?

Each coordinate is a slope for one knob. Together they tell you how to nudge all knobs at once for the largest instantaneous increase.

## Learning as descent

If $L(w)$ is loss and $w$ are parameters:

$$
w_{\text{new}} = w_{\text{old}} - \eta \nabla L(w_{\text{old}})
$$

with step size $\eta > 0$. Too large: overshoot. Too small: crawl.

## Connection to the chain rule

Networks compose functions. The gradient of the composition multiplies local Jacobians (chain rule in higher dimensions). Backprop organizes that bookkeeping.

## Real world

Hill walking in fog with only a local slope measurement is gradient descent as metaphor: you cannot see the global minimum, only the ground under your feet.

## AI / ML bridge

This lesson is the bridge. Linear algebra will represent $w$ and data as vectors. Probability will treat loss as expectation. Calculus supplies the sensitivity.
