## First principles

A **circle** is the set of all points at a fixed distance $r$ (the **radius**) from a center. The **diameter** is $2r$. The **circumference** $C$ and radius are linked by

$$
C = 2\pi r
$$

where $\pi$ is the constant ratio of circumference to diameter for every circle.

<CircleDiagram />

**Area** measures two-dimensional extent. For a circle,

$$
A = \pi r^2
$$

Area scales with the square of length: double the radius, quadruple the area.


## Slow down: curved does not mean unmeasurable

Exhaustion and integrals exist because humans refused to shrug at curves. Bound the mystery between known polygons or rectangles, tighten, and name what remains. $\pi$ is the celebrity constant of that refusal.

## Exhaustion as a method

Archimedes (and earlier Eudoxus-style ideas preserved in Euclid) compared curved regions to polygons that hug them more and more tightly. That is not hand-waving. It is controlled approximation: if every overestimate and underestimate can be forced arbitrarily close, the area is pinned down.

You will meet the same pattern again as Riemann sums in calculus.

## Coordinates preview

In the plane, the circle of radius $r$ centered at the origin is $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$. Set $r = 1$ and you get the **unit circle**, the stage for sine and cosine in the next course.

## Real world

Pizza area vs diameter pricing, track lengths, and cable spool radii are circle arithmetic. Engineers estimate material from area formulas long before they cut metal.

## AI / ML bridge

Constraining vectors to unit length puts them on a sphere (circle in 2D). Angles between those vectors become meaningful similarity scores. Geometry of constrained sets is not optional in modern ML. It is daily language.
