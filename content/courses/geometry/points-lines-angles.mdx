## Why this lesson exists

Numbers answer "how much?" Geometry answers "where?" and "which way?" Once you can place a quantity in space, you can compare shapes, measure turns, and later watch functions move as graphs. Visualization is not decoration. It is a second language for the same truths.

## First principles

A **point** marks a location with no size of its own. A **line** (in the Euclidean plane sense) extends without end in two directions. A **line segment** is the finite piece between two points. A **ray** starts at a point and goes forever in one direction.

An **angle** is formed by two rays that share a vertex. Measuring an angle means measuring how much one ray has turned away from the other.

Euclid's Elements opens with definitions and postulates that try to pin these intuitions down so later theorems do not float free. You do not need every postulate memorized. You need the habit: **name the objects, then argue from agreed rules**.

## Parallel and perpendicular (quick picture)

- Two lines are **perpendicular** when they meet at a right angle (a quarter turn).
- Two lines are **parallel** when they never meet (in the Euclidean plane).

These words become machinery for congruence, similarity, coordinates, and later vectors.

## Real world

Turning a corner is an angle. Aligning a shelf square to a wall is a right angle. Drawing a map scale is turning lengths into drawn lengths while preserving angles when you can.

## AI / ML bridge

Decision boundaries, embedding plots, and "nearest neighbor" all borrow geometric language: points in a space, distances between them, and cuts that separate regions. Geometry trains your eye before the algebra gets high-dimensional.
