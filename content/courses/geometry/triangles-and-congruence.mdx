## First principles

A **triangle** is three points not on one line, joined by three segments. Its interior angles sum to a straight angle ($180^\circ$ or $\pi$ radians) in Euclidean geometry.

<TriangleDiagram />

Why care? Because triangles are **rigid**: if you fix the three side lengths, the shape cannot flex. That is why structures use triangular bracing.

## Congruence

Two figures are **congruent** when one can be moved onto the other by translation, rotation, and reflection (rigid motions), matching every length and angle.

Useful triangle shortcuts (the names are memory aids for which data determine a unique shape up to congruence):

- **SSS:** three sides
- **SAS:** two sides and the included angle
- **ASA / AAS:** two angles and a side (with care about which side)

SSA is not a general congruence shortcut. Ambiguous cases exist.

## Equality of shape vs equality of size

Congruence is geometric equality of size and shape. **Similarity** (next lesson) keeps shape but allows scaling. Keep them separate: "same shape" is not automatically "same size."

## Real world

If two roof panels are congruent, they interchange. Surveyors rebuild inaccessible lengths by building reachable congruent or similar triangles.
