## Two ideas people blur

People say “fraction” and “ratio” as if they were twins. They are relatives, not clones.

- A **fraction** $\frac{a}{b}$ (with $b \neq 0$) is a **number** — a point on the number line — built as $a$ multiplied by the multiplicative inverse of $b$.
- A **ratio** $a : b$ is a **comparison** of two magnitudes of the same kind: how $a$ stands relative to $b$.

You can often encode a ratio as a fraction $\frac{a}{b}$, but the Euclidean tradition is careful: Book V talks about when two comparisons are the same, without first requiring “ratio” to be a completed number object.

> How does this work? Start with same-kind quantities — length with length, mass with mass, count with count. Mixing kinds (“3 meters to 2 kilograms”) is not a ratio in this sense; it is a different animal (a rate with units).

## First principles: parts of a whole

The school fraction $\frac{3}{4}$ says: partition a whole into $4$ equal parts and take $3$ of them. That is the **part–whole** model.

Equivalently, $\frac{3}{4}$ is the number $x$ such that $4x = 3$. That is the **equation** model — and it scales cleanly when you meet algebra.

Equivalent fractions are the same number wearing different clothes:

$$
\frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{8}
$$

because both satisfy the same relationship to $1$, or because cross-multiplying shows $3 \times 8 = 4 \times 6$.

## Ratios: sameness of comparison

Euclid’s *Elements* Book V, Definition 5 (Heath’s tradition; see Joyce’s online Elements) says — in modern paraphrase — that

$$
a : b = c : d
$$

when for every pair of whole numbers $m, n$, the comparison of $ma$ with $nb$ matches the comparison of $mc$ with $nd$ (greater, equal, or less together).

That sounds heavy. The spirit is light: **two ratios are the same when no whole-number test can tell them apart.**

This mattered historically because some lengths are incommensurable — not a ratio of whole numbers — yet still comparable. The diagonal of a unit square and the side are not in a whole-number ratio, but they still have a ratio in Eudoxus’s sense.

## Why “relative size” is a superpower

Absolute size says “$40$.” Relative size says “$40$ out of $200$” — $\frac{1}{5}$. Context changes meaning. A $40$ point exam score is poor or excellent depending on the total. Ratios carry context inside the comparison.

Percentages are ratios with denominator $100$ by convention. Rates like km/h mix kinds and need units — still “relative,” but not a pure Euclidean ratio of like magnitudes.

## Real world

- **Cooking:** dilute $1$ part concentrate with $3$ parts water — ratio $1:3$.
- **Maps:** $1:50{,}000$ means one unit on the map corresponds to $50{,}000$ of the same unit in the world.
- **Medicine:** mg per kg body mass — a designed proportion so dose scales with the patient.

## Mathematician: Eudoxus and the refusal to give up

When Pythagorean hopes that all is whole-number ratio cracked against irrationals, Greek mathematics could have shrunk. Instead, the proportion theory in Book V — linked to Eudoxus — rebuilt comparison on equimultiples. Dedekind, centuries later, would explicitly cite Eudoxus-like ideas while constructing the real numbers.

The ethic: **when a naive story breaks, rebuild the comparison carefully rather than pretending the crack is not there.**

## AI / ML bridge: probability is ratio language

If a dataset has $90$ cats and $10$ dogs, the class fractions are $0.9$ and $0.1$. A classifier’s precision is (true positives) / (predicted positives) — a ratio of counts. Softmax takes scores $z_i$ and produces

$$
p_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}
$$

Each $p_i$ is a relative weight in a whole that sums to $1$. You are not learning “AI magic.” You are watching ratio thinking get normalized.
