## Two ideas people blur

People say “fraction” and “ratio” as if they were twins. They are relatives, not clones.

- A **fraction** $\frac{a}{b}$ (with $b \neq 0$) is a **number** on the line.
- A **ratio** $a : b$ is a **comparison** of two magnitudes of the same kind.

You can often encode a ratio as $\frac{a}{b}$, but Euclid’s Book V is careful: it talks about when two comparisons are the same.

> How does this work? Start with same-kind quantities - length with length, mass with mass. Mixing kinds (“3 meters to 2 kilograms”) is a rate with units, not a pure Euclidean ratio.

## First principles: parts of a whole

The school fraction $\frac{3}{4}$ says: partition a whole into $4$ equal parts and take $3$. Equivalently, $\frac{3}{4}$ is the number $x$ such that $4x = 3$.

<RatioDiagram />

Equivalent fractions are the same relationship wearing different clothes:

$$
\frac{3}{4} = \frac{6}{8}
$$

because $3 \times 8 = 4 \times 6$.

## Ratios: sameness of comparison

Euclid’s Book V, Definition 5 says - in modern paraphrase - that $a : b = c : d$ when for every pair of whole numbers $m, n$, the comparison of $ma$ with $nb$ matches the comparison of $mc$ with $nd$.

The spirit is light: **two ratios are the same when no whole-number test can tell them apart.**

This mattered because some lengths are incommensurable - not a ratio of whole numbers - yet still comparable. The diagonal of a unit square and the side still have a ratio in Eudoxus’s sense.

## Why relative size is a superpower

Absolute size says “$40$.” Relative size says “$40$ out of $200$” - $\frac{1}{5}$. Context changes meaning. Percentages are ratios with denominator $100$ by convention.

## Real world

Cooking dilutions, map scales, medicine doses per kilogram - all designed proportions so one quantity tracks another.

## AI / ML bridge

If a dataset has $90$ cats and $10$ dogs, the class fractions are $0.9$ and $0.1$. Precision is (true positives) / (predicted positives). Softmax produces

$$
p_i = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}
$$

Each $p_i$ is a relative weight in a whole that sums to $1$. Ratio thinking in a suit.

## Carry this forward

- Fractions are numbers; ratios are comparisons.
- Equivalence means the same relative relationship.
- Probability and many metrics speak this language.
