# Sources - Variables and unknowns

## Math & history
- MacTutor: Muḥammad ibn Mūsā al-Khwārizmī (c. 780–850) - *Kitāb al-jabr wa-l-muqābala*; al-jabr (restoration) and al-muqābala (balancing); word “algebra” from al-jabr; “algorithm” from Latinized name; https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Al-Khwarizmi/
- Scholarly note: debate whether he used Euclid directly (Toomer/Rashed vs Gandz) - lesson does not claim Euclidean axiomatic style for his algebra text.

## Mathematician vignette
- House of Wisdom, Baghdad; systematic reduction of problems to standard equation forms; practical + geometric explanations.

## Real world
- Naming an unknown in budgeting, dosing, or engineering (“let the missing length be L”) so relationships can be written before measurement finishes.

## AI / ML bridge
- Features / parameters as named quantities whose values vary across examples or during training: $x$ as input feature, $w$ as learnable weight, $y$ as target - same naming move as classical unknowns, plus optimization over many examples.

## Honesty notes
- Do not mythologize al-Khwārizmī as inventing “the letter x”; symbolic letter algebra evolved later in Europe. Credit the **method of treating unknowns systematically**.
- Diophantus and Indian algebraic traditions also matter historically; this lesson centers al-Khwārizmī because of al-jabr’s conceptual clarity for beginners.
