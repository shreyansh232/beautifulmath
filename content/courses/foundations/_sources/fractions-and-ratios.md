# Sources — Fractions and ratios

## Math & history
- Euclid, *Elements* Book V (esp. Def. 5) — equality of ratios via equimultiples; English guide: David Joyce, Clark University; https://mathcs.clarku.edu/~djoyce/java/elements/bookV/defV5.html
- MacTutor: Eudoxus — theory of ratios set out in Elements Book V; comparable magnitudes; https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Eudoxus/
- UBC notes (Cassels course) on Euclid’s ratio theory as comparing magnitudes without requiring ratios to be “numbers” first; https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~cass/courses/m446-03/ratios.pdf

## Mathematician vignette
- Eudoxus of Cnidus (c. 408–355 BCE) via MacTutor — proportion theory that handles irrationals where earlier Pythagorean approaches failed.

## Real world
- Cooking dilutions, map scales, gear ratios, percentage change — all same-kind comparisons.

## AI / ML bridge
- Probabilities as normalized relative weights; precision/recall as ratios of counts; softmax as turning scores into a ratio distribution over classes. Keep as conceptual bridge, not claiming Euclid invented ML metrics.

## Honesty notes
- Attribution of Book V solely to Eudoxus is traditional (Archimedes attributes an axiom to him); some historians urge caution — lesson attributes the *theory as preserved in Euclid*, associated with Eudoxus.
- Fraction arithmetic (a/b) is the school gateway; ratio theory is deeper and older in the Euclidean sense.
