## First principles

In a finite set of equally likely outcomes,

$$
P(A) = \frac{|A|}{|U|}
$$

favorable over total.

<ProbabilityDiagram />

Probabilities are between 0 and 1. Impossible gets 0, certain gets 1. Disjoint outcomes add.

## Two readings (both useful)

1. **Long-run frequency:** repeat the experiment; the fraction of times $A$ happens approaches $P(A)$ (under ideal assumptions).
2. **Degree of belief:** a coherent assignment of weights to possibilities (subjective Bayesian reading).

Pascal and Fermat's correspondence treated fair games with counting arguments: the combinatorial seed of the first reading.

## Real world

A fair six-sided die has $P(6) = 1/6$. A biased die does not. Data estimates the weights.
