## First principles

An angle measures a **turn**. A full turn around a point returns you to the starting ray.

Two common units:

- **Degree:** a full turn is $360^\circ$. Babylonian sexagesimal habits and Greek astronomy helped lock this convention in place (Hipparchus worked with a 360-part circle).
- **Radian:** a full turn is $2\pi$ radians. One radian is the angle whose arc length equals the radius.

$$
180^\circ = \pi \text{ radians}
$$

Radians make calculus formulas cleaner later because arc length on the unit circle is literally the angle.

## Oriented angles

Trigonometry often treats angles as **oriented**: counterclockwise from the positive $x$-axis is positive in the standard plane convention. That lets sine and cosine become functions of any real input, not only acute triangle corners.

## Real world

Pilots and sailors speak in degrees. Mathematicians and physicists often prefer radians once calculus enters. Both describe the same turn.

## AI / ML bridge

Predicting an angle of $359^\circ$ vs $1^\circ$ looks far apart as raw numbers but is a tiny turn. Encoding angle as $(\cos\theta, \sin\theta)$ avoids that discontinuity. Geometry saves the model from a bad number line.
