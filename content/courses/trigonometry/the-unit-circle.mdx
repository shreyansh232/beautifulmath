## First principles

The **unit circle** is $x^2 + y^2 = 1$. From the positive $x$-axis, turn an oriented angle $\theta$. The landing point $P$ has coordinates

$$
P = (\cos\theta,\ \sin\theta)
$$

<UnitCircleDiagram />

Why radius 1? Because then "adjacent/hypotenuse" collapses to the $x$-coordinate itself, and "opposite/hypotenuse" collapses to $y$. Triangle ratios and circle coordinates become the same objects.

## Signs by quadrant

In the first quadrant both sine and cosine are positive. Elsewhere signs follow the signs of $x$ and $y$. That is how obtuse and reflex angles stay meaningful.

## Pythagorean identity

From $x^2 + y^2 = 1$:

$$
\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = 1
$$

Geometry lesson Pythagoras, rewritten in trig clothing.

## Real world

A wheel's position is an angle. The height of a point on the rim as the wheel turns is a sine wave. Circular motion and oscillation are one picture.

## AI / ML bridge

Rotation by $\theta$ multiplies coordinates by a $2\times 2$ matrix of sines and cosines. Understanding that matrix geometrically makes linear algebra feel less like symbol soup.
