## Periodicity

As $\theta$ increases by $2\pi$, the point on the unit circle returns to the same place. Therefore

$$
\sin(\theta + 2\pi) = \sin\theta, \quad \cos(\theta + 2\pi) = \cos\theta
$$

Plotting $\sin\theta$ against $\theta$ produces a wave that repeats forever.

<SineWaveDiagram />

That is why trigonometry models cycles: the circle is the geometry of repetition.

## Identities as compressed geometry

An **identity** is an equality true for all allowed inputs. The parent identity is still

$$
\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1
$$

Others (angle-addition formulas, double-angle formulas) expand from geometry or algebra on complex exponentials later. Do not memorize a wall of them on day one. Learn what an identity *is*: reusable structure.

## Cosine similarity

For nonzero vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}$,

$$
\cos\phi = \frac{\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|\,\|\mathbf{v}\|}
$$

where $\phi$ is the angle between them. The score is $1$ when they point the same way, $0$ when orthogonal, and $-1$ when opposite.

    This is the same cosine you met on the unit circle, now asked a data question: how aligned are these two directions? Length is stripped away by the denominators so pure scale does not fake similarity.

</StoryNote>

## Real world

Audio synthesizers, signal processing, and seasonal forecasts lean on sinusoidal building blocks. Fourier's insight is that many complicated periodic signals are sums of simpler waves.

## AI / ML bridge

Embedding models map text or images to vectors. Cosine similarity ranks neighbors by direction. Your trig intuition is doing product work.
