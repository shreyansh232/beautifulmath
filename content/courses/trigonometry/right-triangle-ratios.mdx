## First principles

In a right triangle, fix an acute angle $\theta$. Name the sides relative to $\theta$:

- **Hypotenuse:** side opposite the right angle (longest)
- **Opposite:** side across from $\theta$
- **Adjacent:** the non-hypotenuse side that touches $\theta$

<RightTriangleTrigDiagram />

Define:

$$
\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \quad
\cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}, \quad
\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}
$$

Because similar right triangles share angles, these ratios depend on $\theta$, not on how large you draw the triangle.

## Memory aid (with eyes open)

SOH-CAH-TOA is a mnemonic, not an explanation. The explanation is similarity: scale cancels in a ratio of lengths.

## Solving for a length

If you know one side and an acute angle, the definitions rearrange into formulas for the missing sides. Always sketch and label opposite/adjacent before you compute.

## Real world

Surveying, architecture slopes, and game-engine camera pitches use these ratios constantly.
