## First principles

An **equation** asserts equality:

$$
\text{left expression} = \text{right expression}
$$

It is a **truth claim** about quantities. Solving means finding values of the unknowns that make the claim true.

<BalanceDiagram />

If two piles weigh the same, adding the same weight to both keeps them equal. Subtracting the same, or scaling both by the same nonzero factor, does too. That is why we "do the same thing to both sides."


## Slow down: claims, not puzzles

An equation is a claim that two expressions name the same amount. Solving is searching for values that make the claim true. Word problems become kinder when you translate stories into claims before you touch arithmetic.

## Linear equations

A linear equation in one unknown looks like:

$$
ax + b = c
$$

(with $a \neq 0$). Undo from the outside:

1. Subtract $b$: $ax = c - b$
2. Divide by $a$: $x = \frac{c-b}{a}$

You are not performing magic. You are applying invertible transformations.

## Why symbols beat special cases

Writing $ax + b = c$ once covers infinitely many word problems. Viète's push toward literal notation (letters for knowns and unknowns) is the ancestor of that leverage. Al-Khwārizmī earlier taught restoration and balancing in words and geometry. Together they show a through-line: **method first, then numbers**.

## Real world

"Three tickets and a \$4 fee cost \$40" is $3x + 4 = 40$. The story changes; the structure does not.

## AI / ML bridge

A model $\hat{y} = w x + b$ is an equation with parameters $w, b$. Fitting data chooses $w, b$ so predictions sit close to targets. Exact equality becomes "as equal as possible" under a loss.
