## First principles

If $y = f(x)$, the **graph** is the set of points $(x, f(x))$ in the plane.

<LineGraphDiagram />

Coordinates let algebra speak geometrically: the unknown becomes a location.


## Slow down: a graph is a relationship made visible

Every point on a graph is an answer to "when the input is this, the output is that." Clusters, slopes, intercepts, and curves are pattern language. Before you differentiate, you can already see where a graph rises quickly or flattens - calculus will later name those visual hunches.

## Lines

$y = mx + b$ has slope $m$ and intercept $b$. Slope is rise over run between any two points on the line.

Parallel lines share slope. Perpendicular lines in the usual Euclidean plane have slopes whose product is $-1$ (when both are defined).

## What graphs buy you

- Spot intercepts and roots ($f(x) = 0$)
- See growth vs decay
- Compare two functions visually

Descartes's analytic geometry is the historical hinge: equations become curves, curves become equations.

## Real world

A speedometer over time is a graph. Area under a speed curve foreshadows distance (calculus).

## AI / ML bridge

Plotting training loss versus step is reading a function $L(t)$. Sudden spikes, plateaus, and smooth descent are geometric language for optimization behavior.
